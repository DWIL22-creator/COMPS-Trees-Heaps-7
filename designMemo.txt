1 - Why is a tree appropriate for the doctor structure?
Answer: In the doctor structure, a tree is most appropriate because this is a top down process.
The doctor structure represents the reporting hierarchy in the hospital.
There are some doctors that are more professional experience than others, so we would want to reference the top doctor,
which would be the doctor at the root, until we get to the very last doctor.
For instance, a doctor at a higher level on the hierarchy may have the experience to aid a doctor on the lower hierarchy that's assisting a patient with medical advice or to walk them through a medical procedure.
We also want to refrence each doctor or "DoctorNode" from left to right, because that's the order in which we would descent down the hierarchy in the hospital.
I wouldn't just start from the top doctor and then go to the doctor and then go to the bottom most doctor.
That wouldn't make sense unless I have just two doctors in my tree, which would be unrealistic.
Also if some doctors, are not at work, we would want to the next ones up.

3 - How do heaps help simulate real-time systems like emergency intake?
Heaps help to simulate real time systems because patients are helped based on urgency.
A patient that needs a critical surgery would need to be helped more urgently than someone knee pain.
Using the heap system also gives direct access to the top priority, while keeping the patient data sorted.
Once the top patient is helped, the sytem looks at the patients based on the severity of their illness/injury, and helps the next patient with the most need.
This is like patients sitting in a waiting room or a patient waiting to recieve care while they're in the hospital room.
It takes time getting everyone the help they need, and hospitals must prioritize to save as many lives and mitigate effects of an issue before symptoms worsen or become deadly.
